## 2. USB Port Consolidation

### Problem

The baseline schematic faithfully reproduced the development board, which had two separate USB connections:

- **USB Micro-B** — power input only
- **USB Micro-B** — CP2102 UART bridge for debug/flashing

Having two physical USB connectors is impractical for a production device.

### Decision

Consolidated both into a single **USB-C connector** carrying both power and UART.

### USB-C Compliance

USB-C requires 5.1kΩ pull-down resistors on both `CC1` and `CC2` pins to identify the device as a UFP (Upstream Facing Port) drawing power. Without these resistors, only certain chargers will supply VBUS — an issue validated during prototype testing where only specific chargers worked.

| Pin | Resistor     | Purpose            |
|-----|--------------|--------------------|
| CC1 | 5.1kΩ to GND | UFP identification |
| CC2 | 5.1kΩ to GND | UFP identification |

**Reference:** USB Type-C Cable and Connector Specification, Section 4.5.1

### Schematic Change

Removed the separate power USB connector. The USB-C connector carries VBUS (power) and D+/D- (routed to the CP2102 UART bridge), with CC1 and CC2 terminated correctly.
