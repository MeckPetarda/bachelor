# Development Log - February 24, 2026

## Session Summary

This session focused entirely on hardware power delivery - diagnosing, isolating, and resolving a series of interconnected power supply issues affecting stable operation of the ESP32 and UHF RFID reader combination. The session concluded with the completion of Board v2, the first full PCB concept incorporating all design decisions made to date.

---

## 1. Transistor Power Switch - S9013 to BC337 Migration

### Problem

The S9013 NPN transistor used as a low-side switch for RFID reader power control was causing severely degraded RF output power and antenna scan range. When bypassed, the reader performed correctly but the current spikes caused the ESP32 to brownout and restart. USB connection masked the issue by injecting additional supply current.

### Root Cause Analysis

The S9013 V_CE(sat) of 0.6–1.0V at near-maximum collector current (300–380mA load vs 500mA rated max) raised the RFID board GND reference, starving it of effective supply voltage. The BC337 was initially installed in the wrong orientation - S9013 pinout is EBC while BC337 is CBE, making them non-interchangeable pin-for-pin. Operation in inverse active mode confirmed - drastically reduced gain consistent with the observed underpowered reader symptoms. After correcting orientation, base resistor was stepped from 240Ω to 150Ω to drive the BC337 into harder saturation (~17mA base current, forced beta ~22 at 380mA).

| Parameter          | S9013    | BC337-25 | Notes                           |
|--------------------|----------|----------|---------------------------------|
| I_C max            | 500mA    | 800mA    | 60% vs 47% of max at load       |
| V_CE(sat) at load  | 0.6–1.0V | ~300mV   | Lower GND offset                |
| Pinout (flat face) | EBC      | CBE      | Not interchangeable pin-for-pin |
| Base resistor      | 240Ω     | 150Ω     | ~17mA base drive                |

## 2. Power Supply Stabilisation

### Symptom Progression and Diagnosis

With the BC337 correctly installed, intermittent brownout resets persisted during active scanning. Onboard USB-UART convertor was made to run on the board's source by flipping the on-board diode. The VIN pin of the usb-A side of the debug cable was taped as to not mess with the on-board power issues I was trying to debug. (after flipping the diode this is most likely not required anymore, needs testing) Scope measurements on the 5V and 3.3V rails were used to progressively isolate the cause.

*Note*: The board change means that the board cannot now be powered throug the onboard-port and has to be powered from circuit

### Key Findings

- Battery terminal voltage collapsed from 3.5V to 2.7–3.0V under combined load (ESP32 WiFi + RFID reader). This cascaded through the SX1308 boost converter to the 3.3V rail, triggering the ESP32 brownout detector (threshold ~2.5V on VDD3P3_RTC, per TRM Section 9.3.5).
- Root cause confirmed as a partially discharged battery with elevated internal resistance. A fully charged cell (4.2V no-load, minimal droop to 4.1V under full combined load) eliminated the brownout entirely.
- A 1.5Ω series resistor placed on the ESP32 5V feed for supply isolation caused a boot-loop on reset - inrush current during boot created a sustained voltage drop keeping the rail depressed. Resistor removed; capacitance alone is the correct tool at this point in the circuit.
- SX1308 rated output ripple is 50mV under ideal conditions, increasing with load. RFID inventory round current draw confirmed as 18.8ms sustained high-current periods at 30–50ms cycle, not fast spikes - bulk capacitance sizing must account for this timescale.

### Capacitance Deployed

| Location               | Capacitance         | Purpose                                  |
|------------------------|---------------------|------------------------------------------|
| RFID reader VCC input  | 1000µF electrolytic | Bulk reservoir for reader current spikes |
| ESP32 5V VIN           | 1000µF electrolytic | Bulk supply stabilisation                |
| ESP32 3.3V rail        | 100µF electrolytic  | LDO output stabilisation                 |

## 3. Power Delivery Architecture

### Diode OR Configuration

A dual Schottky diode OR configuration was implemented to allow battery backup operation alongside USB 5V primary supply, preventing battery discharge when USB is present and preventing backfeed into the USB source when running on battery.

- Diodes: SS24A (DO-214AC / SMA package), 2A 40V, ~0.3–0.4V forward drop
- Battery path voltage loss: ~0.4V - SX1308 minimum input 2V provides safe margin even at battery minimum of 3.2V

### Fuse Protection

Two SF-1206SP100-2 slow-blow fuses (1A, 63VDC, 1206 package) specified - one per input path, placed after each input source and before the diodes. Slow-blow type selected to tolerate RFID reader power-on inrush without spurious blow.

### Power Switch

SLW-1678105-6A-N-D DPDT slide switch (1A, 12VDC, through-hole) placed after the fuses and before the diode OR junction. Both poles used - one switching the battery path, one switching the USB path - providing complete power isolation of both inputs simultaneously while keeping fuses always in-circuit.

## 4. Battery Voltage Monitoring

GPIO33 (ADC1_CH5) selected for battery cell voltage monitoring. ADC1 confirmed safe for concurrent WiFi operation - ADC2 shares hardware with the WiFi RF subsystem and cannot be used reliably while WiFi is active (per ESP32 TRM).

Voltage divider: R1 = 100kΩ, R2 = 120kΩ scales the 3.2–4.2V cell range to 1.75–2.29V, within ADC1 effective measurement range of 150–2450mV at 11dB attenuation (per ESP32 Datasheet Table 4-4).

## 5. Brownout Detector - Deliberate Disable

The ESP32 brownout detector (CONFIG_ESP_BROWNOUT_DET_LVL = 0, least sensitive) was found to trigger spuriously at phy_init on every boot cycle despite stable 5V and 3.3V rails under scope observation. The transient is sub-microsecond in duration and causes no operational issue but consistently fires the detector at the moment the WiFi RF frontend first powers up.

**Decision:** Brownout detector disabled (CONFIG_ESP_BROWNOUT_DET=n).

**Rationale:** Protection against genuine supply collapse is provided by overlapping hardware mechanisms - TP4056 battery undervoltage cutoff, input fusing, and software battery voltage monitoring via ADC1/GPIO33 with MQTT reporting. This decision should be revisited if power delivery architecture changes in a future board revision.

## 7. Key Lessons Learned

- BJT pinout is manufacturer-dependent - always verify EBC vs CBE before assuming a drop-in replacement is pin-compatible. S9013 (EBC) and BC337 (CBE) are not directly interchangeable.
- Battery state of charge has a dramatic effect on internal resistance and supply stability under pulsed loads. A partially discharged Li-ion cell can collapse under combined ESP32 WiFi + RFID reader demand even when resting voltage appears acceptable.
- Series resistance in a supply rail for decoupling can cause boot-loop conditions if inrush current during reset creates a sustained voltage drop below the brownout threshold. Capacitance alone is the correct decoupling tool at this stage of the circuit.
- The SX1308 shifts to PFM mode at light loads, producing higher amplitude output transients than under full PWM load. This is a consideration for any idle-state power monitoring or reset-sensitive operation.
- RFID inventory rounds produce 18.8ms sustained high-current draw periods, not just fast switching transients. Bulk capacitance must be sized for this timescale.
